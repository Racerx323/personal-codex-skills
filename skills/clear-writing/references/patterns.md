# Patterns and examples

These examples illustrate choices, not replacement rules. Preserve a construction
when it expresses a necessary distinction, a real question, or the writer's voice.

| Pattern | Editing decision |
| --- | --- |
| Empty setup: "Here's the thing", "It is worth noting" | Start with the substantive statement unless the setup carries voice or context. |
| Dramatic reversal: "It is not X. It is Y." | State the point directly when the contrast adds no information. Keep operational boundaries and genuine comparisons. |
| Negative listing and dramatic fragments | Remove theatrical buildup. Keep useful parallel requirements and intentional personal rhythm. |
| Faux insight: "What nobody tells you" | Remove the claim to exclusive insight; retain the supported fact. |
| Inflated importance: "a pivotal moment" | Explain the concrete consequence already established in the source. |
| Superficial analysis: "highlighting our commitment" | Remove the unsupported interpretation or state the demonstrated effect. |
| Vague attribution: "experts agree" | Retain a supplied source, ask for one, or flag the unsupported claim. |
| Synonym cycling | Use the same precise term for the same component. Do not rename a service to avoid repetition. |
| Weak verb phrases: "made a decision" | Use a direct verb when meaning is unchanged. |
| Dramatic colon reveals | Use plain prose when a colon manufactures suspense. Keep labels, lists, and legitimate explanations. |
| Repeated sentence and paragraph shapes | Vary structure only when clarity or natural cadence improves. |
| Stock metaphors and profound endings | Remove an empty closing flourish. Keep a required conclusion or next action. |
| Decorative formatting | Remove emphasis that adds noise. Retain structure that supports scanning or comparison. |
| Broad jargon and filler qualifiers | Replace vague wording with precise language; do not apply a banned-word list to technical terms. |

## Runbook precision

Before: "It is worth noting that the service automatically restarts after failure.
Do not reboot until the backup is verified."

After: "The service automatically restarts after failure. Do not reboot until the
backup is verified."

The adverb describes actual behavior; the negative requirement controls the action.

## Incident uncertainty

Before: "The reality is that the reset may have interrupted the collector.
The cause was not confirmed."

After: "The reset may have interrupted the collector. The cause was not confirmed."

Do not supply a human actor or replace the hypothesis with a causal conclusion.

## Supported specificity

Before: "The change significantly improved throughput."

If measurements are absent, flag the unsupported degree of improvement. Do not
invent a percentage, benchmark, or before-and-after time to make it concrete.

## Personal voice

"Honestly, I liked the clunky old keyboard. It sounded like rain in a biscuit tin."
can remain unchanged. The adverb and metaphor communicate a personal voice.

## Technical literals

Preserve `--dry-run`, `/run/resolvconf`, `HTTP 204`, and a quoted string such as
"What happened?" exactly. A prose style preference does not authorize editing
code, tool output, or a quotation.
